# The rule of meeting two sakin letters ({{a حكمُ التِقاءِ السَّاكِنين}})
## Meeting two sakin letters in one word
Meeting two sakin letters in one word is permissible in two cases:
1) The first of the two sakin letters is a lengthening or soft letter like: {{q 1:7|ٱلضَّ[آ]لِّينَ}}, {{q 68:1|نٓ}} pronounced ({{a نُون}}), {{q 50:1|قٓ}} pronounced ({{a قَاف}}), ({{a عَيْن}}).
2) The second of the two sakin letters has temporary sukoon like: {{q ?|ٱلْعَٰلَمِي[نَ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلْبَيْ[تِ]}}.
## Meeting two sakin letters in two words
Arabs avoid joining two sakin letters in two words; if two sakin letters met during speech, they get rid of them in one of the two following ways:
1) **By dropping the first of them in pronunciation (omitting)**: It occurs when the lengthening letter meets sakin letter after it. So, the lengthening letter and linking hamzah are omitted. Like: {{q 7:22|ذَا[قَا] ٱلشَّجَرَةَ}}, {{q 51:22|وَ[فِى] ٱلسَّمَآءِ}}, {{q 8:32|قَا[لُوا۟] ٱللَّهُمَّ}}.
2) **By giving a vowel**: The origin of eliminating consecutive two sakin letters in two words to pronounce the first of them with **kasrah**, like: {{q 17:110|قُ[لِ] ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ أَ[وِ] ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ}}, {{q 12:31|وَقَالَ[تِ] ٱخْرُجْ}}, {{q 24:50|أَ[مِ] ٱرْتَابُوٓا۟}}.
### It may go against the origin: the first of the two sakin letters may be pronounced
= With fathah, in three forms | a - The preposition (**مِنْ**): {{q ?|مِ[نَ] ٱلشَّٰهِدِينَ}}, {{q ?|مِ[نَ] ٱلْخَٰلِدِينَ}}; (مِنْ) Is structured on sukoon. The first sakin letter (noon) meets the second sakin (laam shamsiyyah) (solar laam) that is merged into sheen b - (Feminine taa) if it is added to it: {{q 66:10|كَانَ[تَا]}}, {{q 41:11|قَالَ[تَآ]}}; In the two words Feminine taa is structured on sukoon and alif of the two is sakinah.

= With fathah (continued) | c- ({{q 3:1|الٓ[مٓ]}} {{q 3:2|ٱللَّهُ}}): the meem is read with fathah at the beginning of Surat Al 'Imran when joining; the meem is an alphabetical letter structured on sukoon (ميمْ) that meets the non-voweled laam of the name of Allah.
= With dammah, in two forms | a - Waaw leen (Soft waaw) that indicates to plural: {{q 2:94|فَتَمَنَّ[وُا۟] ٱلْمَوْتَ}}, {{q 4:42|وَعَصَ[وُا۟] ٱلرَّسُولَ}}; In the two examples:Soft waaw is a sakin letter preceded by fathah. b - Meem of plural: {{q 2:216|عَلَيْكُ[مُ] ٱلْقِتَالُ}}, {{q 10:67|لَكُ[مُ] ٱلَّيْلَ}}; the Meem of plural is a letter structured on sukoon, meeting non-voweled laam of the definite article after dropping the linking hamzah during speech.
