# The rule of meeting two sakin letters ({{a حكمُ التِقاءِ السَّاكِنين}})
## Meeting two sakin letters in one word
Meeting two sakin letters in one word is permissible in two cases:
1) The first of the two sakin letters is a lengthening or soft letter, like: {{q 1:7|ٱلضَّ[آ]لِّينَ}}, {{q 68:1|نٓ}} pronounced ({{a نُون}}), {{q 50:1|قٓ}} pronounced ({{a قَاف}}), ({{a عَيْن}}).
2) The second of the two sakin letters has a temporary sukoon, like: {{q ?|ٱلْعَٰلَمِي[نَ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلْبَيْ[تِ]}}.
## Meeting two sakin letters in two words
Arabs avoid joining two sakin letters in two words; if two sakin letters meet in speech, they get rid of them in one of the two following ways:
1) **By dropping the first of them in pronunciation (omitting)**: it occurs when a lengthening letter meets a sakin letter after it, so the lengthening letter and the linking hamzah are omitted, like: {{q 7:22|ذَا[قَا] ٱلشَّجَرَةَ}}, {{q 51:22|وَ[فِى] ٱلسَّمَآءِ}}, {{q 8:32|قَا[لُوا۟] ٱللَّهُمَّ}}.
2) **By giving a vowel**: the original way of removing two consecutive sakin letters in two words is to pronounce the first of them with **kasrah**, like: {{q 17:110|قُ[لِ] ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ أَ[وِ] ٱدْعُوا۟ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنَ}}, {{q 12:31|وَقَالَ[تِ] ٱخْرُجْ}}, {{q 24:50|أَ[مِ] ٱرْتَابُوٓا۟}}.
### It may go against the origin: the first of the two sakin letters may be pronounced
= With fathah, in three forms | a- The preposition (**مِنْ**): {{q ?|مِ[نَ] ٱلشَّٰهِدِينَ}}, {{q ?|مِ[نَ] ٱلْخَٰلِدِينَ}}; (مِنْ) is built on sukoon, and its sakin noon meets the second sakin letter, the laam shamsiyyah (solar laam) merged into the sheen. b- The feminine taa, when the alif of the dual is added to it: {{q 66:10|كَانَ[تَا]}}, {{q 41:11|قَالَ[تَآ]}}; in the two words the feminine taa is built on sukoon and the alif of the dual is sakinah.

= With fathah (continued) | c- ({{q 3:1|الٓ[مٓ]}} {{q 3:2|ٱللَّهُ}}): the meem is read with fathah at the beginning of Surat Al 'Imran when joining; the meem is an alphabetical letter built on sukoon (ميمْ) that meets the non-voweled laam of the name of Allah.
= With dammah, in two forms | a- Waaw leen (soft waaw) indicating the plural: {{q 2:94|فَتَمَنَّ[وُا۟] ٱلْمَوْتَ}}, {{q 4:42|وَعَصَ[وُا۟] ٱلرَّسُولَ}}; in the two examples the soft waaw is a sakin letter preceded by fathah. b- The meem of the plural: {{q 2:216|عَلَيْكُ[مُ] ٱلْقِتَالُ}}, {{q 10:67|لَكُ[مُ] ٱلَّيْلَ}}; the meem of the plural is a letter built on sukoon, meeting the non-voweled laam of the definite article after the linking hamzah is dropped in connected speech.
