# Rawm, ishmam and sukoon mahd ({{a الرَّومُ والإشمامُ والسُّكونُ المحضُ}})
## 1- Sukoon mahd (pure sukoon)
= Its definition | It is a sukoon devoid of any harakah (vowel); it is used to stop on a letter with **fathah**, **dammah** and **kasrah**.
= Examples | {{q ?|ٱلْعَٰلَمِي[نَ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلرَّحِي[مُ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلدِّي[نِ]}}.
## 2- Rawm
= Its definition | To pronounce part of the vowel (nearly **one third of the harakah**) when stopping on **dammah** or **kasrah**; it can be heard by someone near, not far.
= Examples | {{q ?|نَسْتَعِي[نُ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلرَّحِي[مُ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلدِّي[نِ]}}, {{q 105:1|ٱلْفِي[لِ]}}.
= Rule | When rawm is applied, we follow the same rules of lengthening as when we continue the recitation (the letter of madd is prolonged for 2 counts).
## 3- Ishmam
= Its definition | To circle the lips directly after giving sukoon to the letter with **dammah**, in the same shape as pronouncing a dammah, without any sound; it is not perceived by the blind.
= Examples | {{q ?|نَسْتَعِي[نُ]}}, {{q 11:87|يَٰشُعَيْ[بُ]}}, {{q ?|أَلِي[مٌ]}}.
= Rule | When ishmam is applied, we follow the same rules of lengthening as when we stop with pure sukoon (the letter of madd is prolonged for 2, 4 or 6 counts).

@page 97
### Notice
For Hafs, rawm and ishmam originally occur when stopping at the end of the word; there is neither rawm nor ishmam when continuing the recitation or in the middle of a word, except one word: {{q 12:11|قَالُوا۟ يَٰٓأَبَانَا مَا لَكَ لَا [تَأْمَ۫نَّا] عَلَىٰ يُوسُفَ وَإِنَّا لَهُۥ لَنَٰصِحُونَ}} [Yusuf: 11]. In the narration of Hafs on the authority of 'Asim, it may be pronounced in two ways:
= A- Ishmam | Pronouncing the first noon without a vowel and merging it into the second, circling the lips without a sound directly after starting to pronounce the merged noon, like: ({{a تَأْمَنَّا}}).
= B- Rawm | Keeping the dammah on the first noon and lowering its sound slightly, quickly compared with the neighbouring letters, like: ({{a تَأْمَنُنَا}}).

> **Note** (pronoun haa, according to the detailed opinion, which is preferred): Scholars have three opinions about applying rawm and ishmam to the pronoun haa: 1- It is prohibited in all cases. 2- It is permissible in all cases. 3- In detail: a- they are not applied if the haa is preceded by (**waaw sakinah**, **dammah**, **yaa sakinah** or **kasrah**); b- they are applied if it is preceded by (**alif**, a **sakin sound letter** or **fathah**).
