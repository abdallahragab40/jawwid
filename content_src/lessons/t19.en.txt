# Hamzatul qat' and wasl ({{a هَمزَتا القَطعِ والوَصلِ}})
## a- Hamzatul qat' (dividing hamzah)
= Its definition | It is established (pronounced) at the beginning of speech, when continuing the recitation and when stopping.
= Hamzatul qat' comes | At the beginning of the word, like: {{q 108:1|إِنَّآ [أَ]عْطَيْنَٰكَ ٱلْكَوْثَرَ}} [Al-Kawthar: 1]; in the middle of the word, like: {{q 77:50|فَبِ[أَ]ىِّ حَدِيثٍۭ بَعْدَهُۥ يُ[ؤْ]مِنُونَ}} [Al-Mursalat: 50]; at the end of the word, like: {{q 2:105|وَٱللَّهُ يَخْتَصُّ بِرَحْمَتِهِۦ مَن يَشَآ[ءُ]}} [Al-Baqarah: 105].
= The sign of hamzatul qat' in the Qur'an | ({{a ء}}) above or below the alif.

## b- Hamzatul wasl (linking hamzah)
= Its definition | It is an extra hamzah at the beginning of the word; it is established (pronounced) when starting and dropped when continuing the recitation.
= It is called hamzatul wasl | Because it leads to pronouncing the sakin letter after it, since the origin is not to begin with a sakin letter and not to stop on a voweled letter. So Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad called it **sullam al-lisan** (the ladder of the tongue).
- It can be added to **verbs**, **nouns** and **particles**.
- It is established (pronounced) when starting, like: {{q ?|[ٱ]لْأَرْضِ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]سْتَسْقَىٰ}}.
- It is dropped when continuing, like: {{q ?|وَٱلْأَرْضِ}}, {{q 2:60|وَإِذِ ٱسْتَسْقَىٰ}}, {{q ?|وَٱشْرَبُوا۟}}.
= The sign of hamzatul wasl in the Qur'an | An alif with the head of a tiny s'aad above it.

### How to start with hamzatul wasl (linking hamzah)
### 1- The vowel of the linking hamzah when starting with a verb
- The linking hamzah is pronounced with **dammah** if the third letter has a **permanent dammah**, like: {{q ?|[ٱ]دْخُلُوا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]دْعُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]عْبُدُوا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]سْكُنْ}}.
- The linking hamzah is pronounced with **kasrah** if the third letter is with **kasrah**, like: {{q ?|[ٱ]رْجِعُوٓا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]صْبِرُوا۟}}; with **fathah**, like: {{q ?|[ٱ]بْتَغَىٰ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]خْتَلَفَ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]سْتَكْبَرَ}}; or with a **temporary dammah**, like: {{q ?|[ٱ]مْشُوا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]قْضُوٓا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْتُوا۟}}.
The third letter has a temporary dammah because the command of its singular form is (**امْشِ**), and the sheen takes dammah in the plural form to match the waaw after it; and likewise the rest of the similar verbs.
### 2- The vowel of the linking hamzah in nouns
The linking hamzah in nouns is divided into two sections (standard and heard), and in both of them it is pronounced with **kasrah** when starting with it.
- **Standard** (subject to the rule), like: {{q ?|[ٱ]سْتِكْبَارًا}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]سْتِغْفَارُ}}.
- **Heard** (outside the rule), like: {{q ?|[ٱ]بْنُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]بْنَتَ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]مْرَأَتُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]مْرُؤٌا۟}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ثْنَيْنِ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ثْنَتَيْنِ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]سْمُ}}.
### 3- Adding the linking hamzah to particles
The linking hamzah in particles occurs in the Qur'an as follows:
- Extra and permanent ({{a ال}}), like: {{q ?|[ٱ]لَّذِى}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]لَّتِى}}.
- Extra and non-permanent ({{a ال}}), like: {{q ?|[ٱ]لْعَلِيمُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]لْحَكِيمُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]لرَّحْمَٰنُ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]للَّهُ}}.
In both of them the linking hamzah is pronounced with **fathah** when starting.

> **Notice**: When the reciter stops on the word (**بِئْسَ**) out of necessity or for a test and wants to start with the word (**الاسم**) for a test, he may start with a hamzah with fathah ({{a أَلِسمُ}}), which is better, or start with the laam with kasrah and drop the linking hamzah ({{a لِسمُ}}). When continuing the recitation there is only one way: to drop the linking hamzah and pronounce the laam with kasrah: {{q 49:11|وَلَا تَلْمِزُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ وَلَا تَنَابَزُوا۟ بِٱلْأَلْقَٰبِ بِئْسَ [ٱلِ]ٱسْمُ ٱلْفُسُوقُ بَعْدَ ٱلْإِيمَٰنِ}}.
# Meeting of two qat' hamzahs ({{a إجتماعُ هَمزتي قطعٍ}})
- When two qat' (dividing) hamzahs meet and the second of them is sakinah, it is converted into a lengthening letter matching the vowel of the first hamzah; it is called madd badal (exchange lengthening): {{q ?|ءَ[ا]مَنُوا۟}} its origin ({{a ءَأْمنوا}}), {{q ?|أُ[و]تُوا۟}} its origin ({{a أُؤْتوا}}), {{q ?|إِ[ي]مَٰنًا}} its origin ({{a إِئْمَانًا}}).
- If two voweled qat' hamzahs meet, both of them are pronounced clearly, like: {{q 79:27|[ءَأَ]نتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا}}, {{q 54:25|[أَءُ]لْقِىَ ٱلذِّكْرُ}}, {{q 12:90|[أَءِ]نَّكَ لَأَنتَ يُوسُفُ}}, except the word {{q 41:44|[ءَا۬]عْجَمِىٌّ}} [Fussilat: 44], where only one way is allowed: easing (tasheel) the second hamzah.
= Tasheel (easing) | Pronouncing the hamzah in a way between the hamzah and the lengthening letter matching its vowel: with fathah, between hamzah and alif; with dammah, between hamzah and waaw; with kasrah, between hamzah and yaa.
- Tasheel is mastered only by oral transmission, learning from masterful sheikhs.
- Its sign: a small circle above the alif: {{q 41:44|[ءَا۬]عْجَمِىٌّ}}.

# Hamzatul wasl preceding hamzat qat' with sukoon ({{a دخولُ همزةِ الوَصلِ على همزةِ قطعٍ ساكنة}})
When a linking hamzah precedes a dividing hamzah with sukoon and you want to start with the word, pay attention to:
- **Converting the dividing hamzah** with sukoon into a lengthening letter matching the vowel of the linking hamzah. This case occurs in 7 words in the Qur'an, like: {{q 2:283|[ٱ]ؤْتُمِنَ}}, {{q 46:4|[ٱ]ئْتُونِى}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْذَن}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْتِ}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْتِنَا}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْتِيَا}}, {{q ?|[ٱ]ئْتُوا۟}}.
- {{q 2:283|ٱلَّذِى [ٱؤْ]تُمِنَ}} → ({{a أُوتُمِنَ}}) [Al-Baqarah: 283].
- {{q 46:4|فِى ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ [ٱئْ]تُونِى}} → ({{a إِيتُونِي}}) [Al-Ahqaf: 4].
# Hamzatul qat' preceding hamzatul wasl ({{a دخولُ همزةِ القطعِ على همزةِ الوصلِ}})
= In verbs | When a dividing hamzah precedes a linking hamzah in verbs, the linking hamzah is dropped in writing and pronunciation. This occurs in 7 words in the Qur'an: {{q 19:78|[أَ]طَّلَعَ}}, {{q ?|[أَ]فْتَرَىٰ}}, {{q 37:153|[أَ]صْطَفَى}}, {{q 38:63|[أَ]تَّخَذْنَٰهُمْ}}, {{q 38:75|[أَ]سْتَكْبَرْتَ}}, {{q 63:6|[أَ]سْتَغْفَرْتَ}}. ({{a أَ}}) + ({{a اِسْتَكْبَرْتَ}}) = {{q 38:75|أَسْتَكْبَرْتَ}}, and likewise the rest of the words.
= In particles | When a dividing hamzah precedes the linking hamzah of the laam of the definite article, in these 3 words: {{q 10:51|ءَآلْـَٰٔنَ}}, {{q ?|ءَآللَّهُ}}, {{q ?|ءَآلذَّكَرَيْنِ}}, mentioned in 6 positions in the Qur'an, two ways are allowed:
1) **Ibdal (converting)**: converting the linking hamzah into an alif madd of 6 counts; it is preferred in performance.
2) **Tasheel (easing)**: easing the linking hamzah, pronouncing it between hamzah and alif.
