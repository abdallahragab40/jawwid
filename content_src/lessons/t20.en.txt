# Waqf (stop) ({{a الوَقفُ}})
Science of stop and start is one of the most important researches in the recitation of the Qur'an: the reciter must collect what guides him to a proper stop and good start.
## Comparison among waqf, qat' and sakt
= Waqf (stop) ({{a الوقف}}) | Cutting off the sound at the end of the word for a period of time usually accompanied by taking breath, with an intention of resuming recitation, not abandoning it.
= Qat' (cutting off) ({{a القطع}}) | Interrupting recitation with an intention of ending it, and then doing another job, like ruku' (bowing).
= Sakt (slight pause) ({{a السكت}}) | Cutting off the sound for a period of time less than the time of stop, without taking breath, with an intention of continuing recitation.
## Types of stop
1) **Forced stop** ({{a الاضطراري}}): what happens to the reciter during his recitation due to a necessity forced him to stop, like shortness of breath, coughing or sneezing or other excuses that compel him to stop. **Its rule**: it is permissible to stop at any word until the compelling state passes.
2) **Tested stop** ({{a الاختباري}}): It is required of the reciter to stop at certain word for being examined and ensuring the quality correctness of his recitation. **Its rule**: it is permissible, for education.
3) **Waiting stop** ({{a الانتظاري}}): a stop only for whom combines all different ways of recitation to a sheikh: he stops on a word and then resumes the verse from its beginning until he reads it by all shapes. **Its rule**: Its is permissible on condition of not giving a corrupt meaning.
4) **Optional stopg** ({{a الاختياري}}): the reciter stops by his own choice, with no compelling state. It is divided into:
| Permissible | Impermissible |
| Complete stop ({{a التَّام}}) | Ugly stop ({{a القبيح}}) |
| Sufficient stop ({{a الكافي}}) | |
| Good stop ({{a الحسن}}) | |

## a- Complete stop ({{a الوقفُ التَّام}})
= Its definition | It has complete meaning and is not attached to what follows it, in parsing nor in meaning.
= It is called complete | Because it conveys complete meaning and is not attached to what follows it, in parsing nor in meaning.
= Its rule | It is good to stop on it and start with what follows it.
It occurs most commonly at the end of verses and stories, like stop on Almighty's saying:
- {{q 1:4|مَٰلِكِ يَوْمِ [ٱلدِّينِ]}} {{q 1:5|*}} [Al-Fatihah: 4–5].
- {{q 2:5|أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ عَلَىٰ هُدًى مِّن رَّبِّهِمْ وَأُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ [ٱلْمُفْلِحُونَ]}} {{q 2:6|*}} [Al-Baqarah: 5–6].
- {{q 25:29|لَّقَدْ أَضَلَّنِى عَنِ ٱلذِّكْرِ بَعْدَ إِذْ [جَآءَنِى] وَكَانَ ٱلشَّيْطَٰنُ لِلْإِنسَٰنِ خَذُولًا}} [Al-Furqan: 29].
### Note
Amongst the types of complete stop what is called by scholars as **compulsory stop** (clarifying stop):
= Compulsory stop ({{a الوقفُ اللَّازم}}) | Stopping on a Qur'anic word to make the meaning clear.
= It is rule | It is compulsory to stop on it and start with what follows it. Unless we stop, the meaning may be ambigious in the hearer's mind, so he may not realise the purpose of Allah's words.
Its sign in the Qur'an is meem (**مـ**) above, for example:
- {{q 54:6|فَتَوَلَّ [عَنْهُمْ] يَوْمَ يَدْعُ ٱلدَّاعِ إِلَىٰ شَىْءٍ نُّكُرٍ}} [Al-Qamar: 6].
- {{q 6:36|إِنَّمَا يَسْتَجِيبُ ٱلَّذِينَ [يَسْمَعُونَ] وَٱلْمَوْتَىٰ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ}} [Al-An'am: 36].
- {{q 36:76|فَلَا يَحْزُنكَ [قَوْلُهُمْ] إِنَّا نَعْلَمُ مَا يُسِرُّونَ وَمَا يُعْلِنُونَ}} [Ya-Sin: 76].
- {{q 28:88|وَلَا تَدْعُ مَعَ ٱللَّهِ إِلَٰهًا [ءَاخَرَ] لَآ إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ}} [Al-Qasas: 88].

## b- Sufficient stop ({{a الوقفُ الكافي}})
= Its definition | Stopping on a word that gives correct meaning and is attached to what follows it in meaning, not in parsing.
= It is called sufficient | Because it dispenses with what follows it as they are not attached in parsing, but they are attached in meaning.
= Its rule | It is good to stop on it and start with what follows it, like complete stop.
It occurs at the end of verses and in the middle of them, like stopping on Almighty's saying:
- {{q 2:4|وَٱلَّذِينَ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِمَآ أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ وَمَآ أُنزِلَ مِن قَبْلِكَ وَبِٱلْـَٔاخِرَةِ هُمْ [يُوقِنُونَ]}} {{q 2:5|*}} [Al-Baqarah: 4–5].
- {{q 19:41|وَٱذْكُرْ فِى ٱلْكِتَٰبِ [إِبْرَٰهِيمَ] إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ صِدِّيقًا نَّبِيًّا}} [Maryam: 41].
## c- Good stop ({{a الوقفُ الحسن}})
= Its definition | A stopping that conveys complete meaning by itself and is attached to what follows it in parsing and meaning.
= It is called good | Because it gives understood meaning that make stopping good by itself.
= Its rule | It is good to stop on it, it is not good to start with what follows it except the end of the verse, as stopping at the end of verses is sunnah, whether it is attached to what follows it or not, like:
- **At the end of verses**: {{q 1:2|ٱلْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ [ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ]}} {{q 1:3|*}} [Al-Fatihah: 2–3].
- **Not at the end of verses**: {{q 1:2|ٱلْحَمْدُ [لِلَّهِ] رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ}} [Al-Fatihah: 2].

## d- Ugly stop ({{a الوقفُ القبيح}})
= Its definition | To stop on what does not give complete meaning, because it is attached to what follows it in parsing and meaning; stopping on it gives incomplete or wrong meaning.
= It is called ugly | Due to ugliness of stopping as it: did not give correct meaning, or gave an unintended meaning, or deluded with a corrupt meaning.
= Its rule | The reciter shouldn't stop on it deliberately except for urgency; if he had to stop, he should resume (until giving a correct meaning).
- Stopping on a word that doesn't give a meaning because it is strongly attached to what follows it in parsing and meaning, like: {{q 1:2|[ٱلْحَمْدُ] لِلَّهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَٰلَمِينَ}} [Al-Fatihah: 2].
- Stopping on a word that deludes with a meaning unlike Allah's intention, like: {{q 4:43|لَا تَقْرَبُوا۟ [ٱلصَّلَوٰةَ] وَأَنتُمْ سُكَٰرَىٰ}} [An-Nisa': 43].
- Stopping on a word which deludes with an inappropriate meaning for Allah, like: {{q 47:19|فَٱعْلَمْ أَنَّهُۥ [لَآ إِلَٰهَ] إِلَّا ٱللَّهُ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنۢبِكَ وَلِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَٱلْمُؤْمِنَٰتِ}} [Muhammad: 19].

# Symbols of stop in the Qur'an ({{a علاماتُ الوقفِ في المُصحفِ}})
= {{a مـ}} | Symbol of compulsory stop: {{q 6:36|إِنَّمَا يَسْتَجِيبُ ٱلَّذِينَ [يَسْمَعُونَ] وَٱلْمَوْتَىٰ يَبْعَثُهُمُ ٱللَّهُ ثُمَّ إِلَيْهِ يُرْجَعُونَ}} [Al-An'am: 36].
= {{a لا}} | Symbol of prohibited stop, because of incomplete: {{q 39:33|وَٱلَّذِى جَآءَ بِٱلصِّدْقِ وَصَدَّقَ [بِهِۦٓ] أُو۟لَٰٓئِكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُتَّقُونَ}} [Az-Zumar: 33].
= {{a ج}} | Symbol of possibility of both stopping and continuing: {{q 10:65|وَلَا يَحْزُنكَ قَوْلُهُمْ إِنَّ ٱلْعِزَّةَ لِلَّهِ [جَمِيعًا] هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ}} [Yunus: 65].
= {{a قلى}} | Symbol of possibility of continuing while stopping is better: {{q 2:191|فَإِن قَٰتَلُوكُمْ فَٱقْتُلُوهُمْ كَذَٰلِكَ جَزَآءُ ٱلْكَٰفِرِينَ}} [Al-Baqarah: 191].
= {{a صلى}} | Symbol of possibility of stopping while continuing is better: {{q 67:3|مَّا تَرَىٰ فِى خَلْقِ ٱلرَّحْمَٰنِ مِن [تَفَٰوُتٍ] فَٱرْجِعِ ٱلْبَصَرَ هَلْ تَرَىٰ مِن فُطُورٍ}} [Al-Mulk: 3].
= {{a ∴ ∴}} | Symbol of related (embracing) stop: if the reader stops on one of the two positions, he should not stop on the other: {{q 2:2|ذَٰلِكَ ٱلْكِتَٰبُ لَا [رَيْبَ] [فِيهِ] هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ}} [Al-Baqarah: 2].
- It is also permissible to join the two positions together with no stopping on any of them.
