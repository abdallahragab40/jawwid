# Idgham in the Holy Qur'an ({{a الإدغامُ في القرآنِ الكريم}})
= Definition | To insert non-voweled letter into a voweled letter where they become one stressed letter as the second one.
= Benefit | To pronounce letters easily, as pronouncing one single merged letter is lighter than pronouncing two separate ones.
= Causes | two identical letters, two close letters and two similar Letters meeting.
## Divisions
### Idgham kabir (big merging)
inserting voweled letter into another voweled letter to become one stressed (mushaddad) letter as second.
- Idgham kabir is not applied by Hafs on the authority of Asim.
- It is called Idgham kabir due to the multiple steps involved replacing the first letter if not identical to the second, then making it non-voweled, and finally merging it with the second letter.
### Idgham saghir (small merging)
Inserting non-voweled letter into voweled letter to become one mushaddad letter as the second.
- It is called idgham saghir due to fewer steps involved, compared to idgham kabir.
- Idgham saghir is divided into two sections in terms of characteristic:
= Complete idgham | Merging the articulation point and characteristic of the letter into the next. The merged letter is removed completely and has no traces when pronouncing. Like: {{q 61:14|فَـَٔامَنَ[ت طَّ]آئِفَةٌ}}, {{q 2:256|قَ[د تَّ]بَيَّنَ}}, {{q 111:5|مِّ[ن مَّ]سَدٍۭ}}. Its sign: The first letter is free from Sukoon and the second is mushaddad.

@page 74
= Incomplete idgham | Merging the articulation point of the letter not characteristic into the next. The merged letter is removed, but some of its characteristics are kept. Incomplete Idgham does not occur except:
- Merging noon sakinah or tanween into **yaa** or **waaw**, like: {{q ?|وَمَ[ن يَ]عْمَلْ}}, {{q ?|مِّ[ن وَ]رَآئِهِم}}, {{q 78:13|سِرَاجً[ا وَ]هَّاجًا}}, {{q 90:3|وَوَالِ[دٍ وَ]مَا وَلَدَ}}. The characteristic of merged letter (ghunnah) is preserved.
- Merging **t'aa** into **taa**, like: {{q 5:28|بَسَ[طت]}}, {{q 27:22|أَحَ[طتُ]}}.
- Its sign: The first letter is free from Sukoon and the second letter is not mushaddad.

# The relationships between two meeting letters ({{a مَبحَثُ عِلاقاتِ الحُروفِ}})
Every two letters meet in pronouncing or writing, there is a relationship between them. So, they may be identical, similar, close or far apart. This meeting may be in one word or between two words. In the following, we will define every type of this meeting and clarify its rule.
## 1- Two identical letters ({{a الحَرفانِ المُتماثِلان}})
These letters share the same articulation point and characteristics like: {{q 5:61|وَقَ[د دَّ]خَلُوا۟}}, {{q 2:2|فِي[هِ هُ]دًى}}, {{q 24:35|تَمْ[سَسْ]هُ}}.
The condition of Idgham (merging) two identical letters: The first of the two identical letters is sakin and is not a lengthening letter like:
- **Merging baa into baa**: {{q 2:60|ٱضْرِ[ب بِّ]عَصَاكَ ٱلْحَجَرَ}} [Al-Baqarah: 60].
- **Merging taa into taa**: {{q 2:16|رَبِحَ[ت تِّ]جَٰرَتُهُمْ}} [Al-Baqarah: 16].
- **Merging daal into daal**: {{q 5:61|وَقَ[د دَّ]خَلُوا۟}} [Al-Ma'idah: 61].
- **Merging thaal into thaal**: {{q 21:87|إِ[ذ ذَّ]هَبَ}} [Al-Anbiya': 87].
- **Merging raa into raa**: {{q 18:24|وَٱذْكُ[ر رَّ]بَّكَ}} [Al-Kahf: 24].
- **Merging noon into noon**: {{q 30:29|مِّ[ن نَّ]ٰصِرِينَ}} [Ar-Rum: 29].
- **Merging faa into faa**: {{q 17:33|فَلَا يُسْرِ[ف فِّ]ى ٱلْقَتْلِ}} [Al-Isra': 33]; was mentioned once in the Qur'an.
- **Merging laam into laam**: {{q 25:10|وَيَجْعَ[ل لَّ]كَ قُصُورًۢا}} [Al-Furqan: 10].
- **Merging 'ain into 'ain**: {{q 18:78|تَسْتَطِ[ع عَّ]لَيْهِ}} [Al-Kahf: 78]; was mentioned once in the Qur'an.
- **Merging meem into meem**: {{q 106:4|أَطْعَمَهُ[م مِّ]ن جُوعٍ}} [Quraysh: 4]; idgham shafawi (oral merging).
- **Merging kaaf into kaaf**: {{q 4:78|يُدْرِ[ككُّ]مُ ٱلْمَوْتُ}} [An-Nisa': 78].
- **Merging haa into haa**: {{q 24:33|يُكْرِ[ههُّ]نَّ}} [An-Nur: 33].
- **Merging waaw into waaw**: {{q 83:3|أَ[و وَّ]زَنُوهُمْ}} [Al-Mutaffifin: 3].
- **Merging yaa into yaa**: {{q 68:6|بِأَ[يي]كُمُ}} [Al-Qalam: 6]; it is mentioned once.
If the first of the two identical letters is voweled or it is a lengthening letter, It will not be merged into the next letter (Merging is not applied) like: {{q 24:35|تَمْ[سَسْ]هُ}}, {{q ?|يَعْلَ[مُ مَ]ا بَيْنَ}}, {{q ?|قَالُ[وا۟ وَ]هُمْ}}, {{q ?|فِ[ى يُ]وسُفَ}}, {{q 2:2|فِي[هِ هُ]دًى}}.

## 2- Two similar letters ({{a الحَرفانِ المُتجانسان}})
These letters share the same articulation point and differ in some characteristics like: {{q 43:89|فَٱصْفَ[حْ عَ]نْهُمْ}}, {{q 81:7|ٱلنُّفُو[سُ زُ]وِّجَتْ}}, {{q 37:125|أَ[تَدْ]عُونَ}}.
Similarity is one of the reasons for Idgham (merging), but meeting two similar letters does not mean that they must be merged, The reciters do not merge every similar letters, but they merge certain letters, Imam Hafs made Idgham of two similar letters limited to 7 cases provided that the first of them is sakin:
### a- Articulation point of th'aa, thaal and thaa ({{a ظ، ذ، ث}})
1) **Merging thaal into th'aa**, in 2 Positions: {{q 4:64|إِ[ذ ظَّ]لَمُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَهُمْ}} [An-Nisa': 64], {{q 43:39|إِ[ذ ظَّ]لَمْتُمْ}} [Az-Zukhruf: 39].
2) **Merging thaa into thaal**, in 1 Position: {{q 7:176|يَلْهَ[ث ذَّ]ٰلِكَ}} [Al-A'raf: 176].
### b- Articulation point of baa and meem ({{a ب، م}})
3) **Merging baa into meem**, in 1 Position: {{q 11:42|يَٰبُنَىَّ ٱرْكَ[ب مَّ]عَنَا}} [Hud: 42].
### c- Articulation point of t'aa, daal and taa ({{a ط، د، ت}})
4) **Merging daal into taa**: {{q 2:256|قَ[د تَّ]بَيَّنَ}} [Al-Baqarah: 256].
5) **Merging taa into daal**, in 2 Positions: {{q 7:189|أَثْقَلَ[ت دَّ]عَوَا ٱللَّهَ}} [Al-A'raf: 189], {{q 10:89|أُجِيبَ[ت دَّ]عْوَتُكُمَا}} [Yunus: 89].
6) **Merging taa into t'aa**: {{q 61:14|فَـَٔامَنَ[ت طَّ]آئِفَةٌ}} [As-Saff: 14].
7) **Merging t'aa into taa**, in 4 words: {{q 5:28|بَسَ[طت]}}, {{q 27:22|أَحَ[طتُ]}}, {{q 39:56|فَرَّ[طتُ]}}, {{q 12:80|فَرَّ[طت]مْ}}.
> **Note**: Merging t'aa into taa is Incomplete Idgham because t'aa is stronger than taa and strong is not merged into weak. The characteristics of t'aa are preserved including Itbaq (adhesion), but qalqalah (disturbance) is removed.

## 3- Two close letters ({{a الحَرفانِ المُتقاربان}})
These letters are close to each other in the articulation point and characteristics.
- close to each other in the articulation point and characteristic like: {{q ?|مِّ[ن لَّ]دُنْهُ}}.
- close to each other in the articulation point and different in characteristic like: {{q 23:112|عَدَ[دَ سِ]نِينَ}}.
- close to each other in the characteristic and different in articulation point like: {{q ?|لَّقَ[دْ كَ]انَ لَكُمْ}}.
Closeness is one of the reasons for Idgham, but meeting two close letters doesn't mean that they must be merged. The reciters do not merge every two close letters, but they merge certain letters. Imam Hafs made Idgham of two close letters:
1) **Merging laam into raa**: {{q 4:158|بَ[ل رَّ]فَعَهُ ٱللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ}} [An-Nisa': 158], {{q 21:56|بَ[ل رَّ]بُّكُمْ}} [Al-Anbiya': 56], {{q 23:29|وَقُ[ل رَّ]بِّ أَنزِلْنِى مُنزَلًا مُّبَارَكًا}} [Al-Mu'minun: 29].
- They are similar letters, according to the school of thought of Al-Far'aa. It is a required Idgham of close letters.
- There is an exclusion: {{q 83:14|بَ[لْ رَ]انَ}}; Merging laam into raa is not applied due to sakt (slight pause without taking breath) that prevents Idgham.
2) **Merging qaaf into kaaf**, Almighty's word: {{q 77:20|أَلَمْ نَخْلُ[قكُّ]م مِّن مَّآءٍ مَّهِينٍ}} [Al-Mursalat: 20].
- The reciters differed about remaining the characteristic of qaaf or not, It is permissible to apply complete Idgham or Incomplete Idgham and the first (complete Idgham) is recited by Hafs.
3) **Merging laam shamsiyyah (solar laam)** in 13 letters; it will be clarified in the subject of the laam of the definite article.
4) **Merging noon sakinah and tanween** in 5 letters of the word (**يَرْمُلُو**); It was mentioned in the subject of noon sakinah and tanween.

## 4- Two far-apart letters ({{a الحَرفانِ المُتباعِدان}})
These letters are far apart in the articulation point and characteristics like: {{q ?|تُ[حْمَ]لُونَ}}, {{q ?|تَ[شْكُ]رُونَ}}.
= Its rule | Ithhar (clarity) in all recitations.
### Note 1: Some classifiers divide two identical, similar or close letters into
| | Definition | Examples | Its rule |
| Saghir (small) | The first letter is sakin and the second is voweled | | It was clarified above |
| Kabir (big) | Both letters are voweled | {{q 2:2|فِي[هِ هُ]دًى}}, {{q 81:7|ٱلنُّفُو[سُ زُ]وِّجَتْ}}, {{q ?|رِزْ[قُكُ]مْ}} | Ithhar (clarity) by Hafs |
| Mutlaq (absolute) | The first letter is voweled and the second is sakin | {{q 24:35|تَمْ[سَسْ]هُ}}, {{q 37:125|أَ[تَدْ]عُونَ}}, {{q 2:120|وَلَ[ن تَ]رْضَىٰ}} | Ithhar must be applied by all reciters |
### Note 2: A number of scholars said that Hafs recited with idgham kabir in a few positions, like
- {{q 12:11|تَأْ[مَ۫نَّا]}} [Yusuf: 11], Its origin ({{a تأمَنُنَا}}).
- {{q 18:95|مَكَّ[نِّى]}} [Al-Kahf: 95], Its origin ({{a مكنَنِي}}).
- {{q 39:64|تَأْمُرُ[وٓنِّىٓ]}} [Az-Zumar: 64], Its origin ({{a تأمُرُونَنِي}}).
- {{q 6:80|أَتُحَٰٓجُّ[وٓنِّى]}} [Al-An'am: 80], Its origin ({{a أتحاجُّونَنِي}}).
- {{q 4:58|نِ[عِمَّا]}} [An-Nisa': 58], Its origin ({{a نعمَ مَا}}).
- {{q 10:35|يَ[هِدِّىٓ]}} [Yunus: 35], its origin ({{a يهْتَدي}}).
It is called idgham kabir when we look at the origin of the word; in its current form, it is not called idgham.
