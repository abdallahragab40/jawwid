# Heaviness and lightness ({{a التَّفْخِيمُ والتَّرقِيقُ}})
This research is strongly linked to the articulation points of letters and their characteristics. Its issue is dividing Arabic letters into heavy and light and, then emitting letter with the deserved characteristic according to its heaviness or light.
## Heaviness
= In linguistics | Fatness.
= In applied definition | fatness that enters the body of the letter so, the mouth is filled with its echo.
= Examples | {{q ?|ٱلْ[غَ]يْبِ}}, {{q ?|[خَا]لِصَةً}}, {{q ?|بَ[قْ]لِهَا}}.

## Lightness
= In linguistics | Thinness.
= In applied definition | thinness that enters the body of the letter so, the mouth is not filled with its echo.
= Examples | {{q ?|أَ[كْ]ثَرُهُمْ}}, {{q ?|أَ[تْ]رَابًا}}, {{q ?|أَ[شْ]تَاتًا}}.

### Heavy and light letters can be divided into three sections
- Letters that are always heavy: ({{a خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ}}).
- Letters that are sometimes heavy and sometimes light: (**alif**, the **laam** of the exalted name ({{a الله}}) or ({{a اللهم}}), and **raa**).
- Letters that are always light: **the rest of the alphabetical letters**.

@page 55
# Letters that are always heavy ({{a حروفٌ مُفخَّمَةٌ دائمًا}})
Scholars of Tajweed agree that the letters that are always heavy are the letters of ist'laa (letters of elevation) ({{a خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ}}), With no exclusion whether they are voweled or non-voweled and whether they are adjacent to independent letter or other, These letters vary in the degree of tafkheem (heaviness), Heaviness of the letter is determined by its elevation so, the more the letter is elevated, the more it is.
- **Ordering of the elevated letters according to the strength of heaviness**: the strongest letter is {{a ط}}, and then {{a ض، ص، ظ، ق، غ، خ}}.
### Ranks of heaviness for the elevated letters
= The first school of thought | They made them three ranks and Ibn At'haan Al-Andalusy chose it: - Letter is with Fathah. - Letter is with dammah. - Letter is with kasrah. Non-voweled letter has no separated rank, but it follows the rank of the Vowel on the preceding letter.
= The second school of thought | They made them five ranks, and Ibn Al-Gazari chose it.
1) The first rank is letter with fathah followed by Alif Madd.
2) The second rank is letter with Fathah and not followed by Alif Madd.
3) The Third rank is letter with dammah.
4) The fourth rank is letter with Sukoon.
5) The fifth rank is letter with kasrah.

# Letters that are always light ({{a حروفٌ مرقَّقةٌ دائمًا}})
They are the letters of istifaal (lowering) except Alif Madd, laam of the exalted name and raa that are sometimes light and sometimes heavy.

@page 57
### Cases of heavy raa
1) Raa is with Fathah like: {{q ?|[رَ]مَضَانَ}}, {{q ?|ٱلْقُ[رَ]ىٰ}}.
2) Raa is with sukoon and preceded by letter with Fathah like: {{q ?|مَ[رْ]يَمَ}}, {{q ?|نَ[رْ]فَعُ}}.
3) Raa is with conditional Sukoon (due to stopping on it) and preceded by letter with Sukoon (not Yaa) and the original Sukoon is preceded by Fathah like: {{q ?|وَٱلْفَجْ[رِ]}}, {{q ?|وَٱلْوَتْ[رِ]}}.
4) Raa is with dammah like: {{q ?|[رُ]زِقُوا۟}}, {{q ?|تَعْبُ[رُ]ونَ}}.
5) Raa is with Sukoon and preceded by letter with dammah like: {{q ?|ٱلْقُ[رْ]ءَانُ}}.
6) Raa is with conditional Sukoon and preceded by letter with Sukoon and the original sukoon is preceded by letter with dammah like: {{q ?|ٱلْغَفُو[رُ]}}, {{q ?|خُضْ[رٍ]}}.
7) Raa is with Sukoon and preceded by letter with kasrah and followed by heavy letter not with kasrah in the same word, They are five words in the Qur'an: {{q ?|قِ[رْ]طَاسٍ}}, {{q ?|وَإِ[رْ]صَادًا}}, {{q ?|مِ[رْ]صَادًا}}, {{q ?|لَبِٱلْمِ[رْ]صَادِ}}, {{q ?|فِ[رْ]قَةٍ}}.
8) Raa is with sukoon and preceded by conditional kasrah whether it is pronounced or implied like: {{q ?|ٱ[رْ]جِعُوٓا۟}}, {{q ?|ٱ[رْ]تَبْتُمْ}}, {{q ?|أَمِ ٱ[رْ]تَابُوٓا۟}}, {{q ?|رَّبِّ ٱ[رْ]حَمْهُمَا}}.
**Briefly**, When hamzatul wasl (Linking Hamzah) comes before raa sakinah, raa is heavy.
### Cases of light raa
1) raa is with kasrah like: {{q ?|[رِ]يحٌ}}, {{q ?|يَعْ[رِ]فُونَهَا}}.
2) Raa is with original Sukoon and preceded by original kasrah and is not followed by heavy letter like: {{q ?|فِ[رْ]عَوْنَ}}, {{q ?|يَغْفِ[رْ]}}.
3) Raa is with conditional sukoon (because of stopping) and preceded by Sukoon and the original Sukoon is preceded by kasrah like: {{q ?|حِجْ[رٍ]}}, {{q ?|ٱلسِّحْ[رَ]}}.
4) Raa is with Sukoon and preceded by Yaa sakinah (Yaa Madd or Yaa leen) like: {{q ?|بَصِي[رٌ]}}, {{q ?|خَيْ[رٌ]}}.

### Heaviness and lightness are allowed
### {{q 26:63|فِرْقٍ}} in continuing recitation
If raa is with sukoon and preceded by letter with kasrah and is followed by elevated letter with kasrah Allah says: {{q 26:63|فَكَانَ كُلُّ فِ[رْ]قٍ كَٱلطَّوْدِ ٱلْعَظِيمِ}} [Ash-Shu'ara: 63]. It is mentioned in only one position in the Qur'an.
- **Note 1**: When we stop on Qaaf with sukoon, raa must be heavy because the reason for lightness was removed, It (lightness) was conditional on pronouncing the next elevated letter (qaaf) with kasrah and it became with sukoon.
- **Note 2**: If we stop on the qaaf with rawm, the raa can be heavy or light (both rules are possible).
### {{q 12:99|مِصْرَ}}, {{q 34:12|ٱلْقِطْرِ}} when stopping
Raa with sukoon is preceded by elevated letter with Sukoon and the elevated letter is preceded by kasrah Allah says:
- {{q 12:99|وَقَالَ ٱدْخُلُوا۟ مِصْ[رَ] إِن شَآءَ ٱللَّهُ ءَامِنِينَ}} [Yusuf: 99]. It is mentioned 4 times in the Qur'an [Yunus: 87; Yusuf: 21 and 99; Az-Zukhruf: 51].
- {{q 34:12|وَأَسَلْنَا لَهُۥ عَيْنَ ٱلْقِطْ[رِ] وَمِنَ ٱلْجِنِّ}} [Saba': 12]. It is mentioned one time in the Qur'an.
- **Note 1**: The Raa is heavy if we continued recitation because there is Fathah above it in the word {{q 12:99|مِصْ[رَ]}}, Raa is light if we continued recitation because there is kasrah below it in the word {{q 34:12|ٱلْقِطْ[رِ]}}.
- **Note 2**: Imam Ibn Al-Gazari chose heaviness when we stop on raa with sukoon in the word {{q 12:99|مِصْ[رَ]}}, because it is heavy when continuing recitation. He chose lightness when we stop on raa with sukoon in the word {{q 34:12|ٱلْقِطْ[رِ]}}, because it is light when continuing recitation.

@page 59
- **Note 3**: Some scholars make both heaviness and lightness of raa are permissible in the next three words when stopping, but the priority is to be light. In Allah's saying:
- {{q 54:16|فَكَيْفَ كَانَ عَذَابِى وَنُذُ[رِ]}} [Al-Qamar: 16].
- {{q 89:4|وَٱلَّيْلِ إِذَا يَسْ[رِ]}} [Al-Fajr: 4].
- {{q 26:52|وَأَوْحَيْنَآ إِلَىٰ مُوسَىٰٓ أَنْ أَسْ[رِ] بِعِبَادِىٓ إِنَّكُم مُّتَّبَعُونَ}} [Ash-Shu'ara: 52].
This is when the raa is with sukoon due to stopping and is followed by an omitted yaa. There are two opinions: whoever makes the raa **light** takes into account its origin where yaa is omitted and also when continuing recitation. Whoever makes raa **heavy** heavy, he does not regard its origin nor continuaton of recitation so, it is heavy according to the rule.
