# Jawwid learner pilot

Prepared 14 November 2026. This is a ready-to-use research plan, not a claim that a pilot has run.

## Prerequisites

Select a coherent expert-reviewed lesson/practice path in each pilot language and identify its release build. Confirm the rights dependencies in `release-dependencies.md` for the distribution being used. Preview builds can support supervised interface testing with content clearly labelled draft; do not treat their scores or material as validated teaching.

Recruit about 12–20 adult volunteers across the supported languages and beginner/revision needs, plus qualified Tajweed teachers. A small sample can expose usability problems; it cannot establish representative retention or recitation competence. Recruitment and messages require the owner's action; no invitations have been sent.

## Opening script

“We are testing the app, not you. You can stop or skip a task at any time. We would like to note which tasks work, where you get stuck, and your suggestions. The app has no remote analytics. Please do not share private notes, recordings, or a full study backup. May we record task completion and your comments under a participant code?”

Record consent in the research log; recordings require separate permission. Use participant codes rather than names or email addresses in the results sheet. Agree a retention/deletion date before collecting pilot records.

## First session — approximately 20 minutes

Ask the learner to think aloud and provide help only after noting the point of difficulty.

1. Choose a language and start without creating an account. Ask what they expect the study preference to change.
2. Find and begin the reviewed starter lesson. Ask them what the lesson covers and how long they expect it to take.
3. Use Contents, add a note to a section, leave the lesson and return to that section from Notes.
4. Complete a short check. Ask the learner to explain the feedback and the difference between lesson completion and answering quiz questions.
5. Leave practice partway through, close/reopen the app, and continue saved practice. Confirm no answer is submitted twice.
6. Save a rule, compare two related rules, and return to the original source page.
7. Search for a term in their own words, reopen a recent search, and clear search history.
8. Ask whether they want a reminder; let them decline. If they opt in, schedule a convenient time and verify delivery on their own device.
9. Demonstrate backup only with dummy notes. Confirm the replacement warning is understood; do not request a private full backup.

For each task record: completed independently / completed with help / not completed, approximate duration, confusion and the learner's wording. Record bugs separately with build, language, theme, text scale and content identifier.

## Follow-up

Ask learners to keep a voluntary daily log for 14 days: whether they learned that day, whether a lesson/check/review was completed, whether they used a reminder, and one optional comment. On days seven and fourteen, ask what prompted return or prevented use. Review a sample of recitation in person with a qualified teacher if practical-learning claims are being investigated; quiz results alone are insufficient.

## Measures and decisions

- Activation: first meaningful lesson/check completed / all consenting participants who started.
- First-session usability gate: proposed target at least 8 of 10 observed users complete the starter activity without help; the threshold is a prototype decision, not a measured baseline.
- D7 return: learning activity reported on the seventh calendar day after activation / activated participants whose seventh day has elapsed. Report missing logs separately; do not silently count them as return or non-return.
- Weekly consistency: number of learning days in each seven-day window, using the same answer/completion definition as the app's weekly goal.
- Delayed recall: reviewed questions answered correctly after a gap, reported separately from immediate retries and lesson completion. Stage-based app summaries are question counts, not independent accuracy studies.
- Segment results by language and study preference where numbers permit; show numerator, denominator and sample limitations.

Prioritize repeated blockers before more content breadth. Decide whether to expand audio only after learner/teacher feedback supports the one-unit audio workflow and recording rights/reviewer capacity are available. The app itself does not aggregate these metrics remotely.
